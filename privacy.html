Privacy policy

Rocket Rover for iPhone (Android to follow) · Last updated 11 November 2026 (anonymous statistics, “Write to us”, links and install source from version 1.0.2)

Publisher: Vibalyze OÜ, Järvevana tee 9, 11314 Tallinn, Estonia (registry code 17240903) · Contact: vibalyze@zohomail.eu

Rocket Rover collects no personal data. There is no account and no login, there are no ads, and your garage, records and trophies are saved on your own phone. From version 1.0.2 the game sends a few anonymous usage statistics so we can see which parts of it work, and you can switch them off in Settings. If you write to us from the game, we receive your message. Purchases are handled by Apple (or Google Play on Android).

Who we are

What the game sends (version 1.0.2 and later)

Anonymous usage statistics. The game sends short gameplay events to our own server, for example “race won by 1.5 seconds”, “bet: fair”, “flew to planet 2” or “badge earned”. Each event holds numbers and fixed labels chosen by the game, plus the app version, the operating system and its version, your phone’s language and time zone offset, and the country of the connection.

  • Events are tied only to a random install ID made on your phone (and a random ID per session). It is not your name, not your Apple or Google account, not a device identifier and not the advertising identifier. A new random ID replaces it after 13 months.
  • The server derives the country from the connection; it never stores or logs your IP address.
  • Raw events are deleted after 120 days. Daily totals that hold no IDs are kept for 25 months.
  • Offline, events wait on the phone (at most 1,000) and are sent later. The game works fully without a connection.
  • Turn it off any time: in Settings, tap Privacy and switch off “Share anonymous stats”. Off deletes the events still waiting and nothing more is sent.

Anonymous error reports. If the game hits a software error, it sends an “error” event with the kind of error, a short fingerprint, and the names of the game’s own code files and lines where it happened, so we can find and fix the bug. The error’s message is never sent, and nor is anything else from your phone. These reports go through the same anonymous statistics, with the same install ID, retention and off switch.

Purchases. When you buy or restore something, the statistics note which product it was, its price and currency. The payment itself is handled entirely by Apple or Google; we never see your payment details or your account.

Where the install came from (iPhone only). On first launch the game asks Apple’s AdServices framework for a token and sends it to our server once, so we can learn whether the install came from an Apple Search Ads campaign (campaign, ad group and keyword, the country and the date). It contains no advertising identifier and nothing about you, it is not tracking, and the game shows no tracking prompt. If statistics are off, nothing is sent.

Links and install source. If the game is opened from a link, the statistics note which kind of link it was (for example the campaign name in it), never the full link. On Android the game reads once which store campaign the install came from (Google Play’s install referrer: campaign names only, never an ad click ID). On iPhone the game tells iOS how far an install got (for example “finished the intro”) so Apple’s SKAdNetwork can report anonymous, aggregated campaign results; Apple sends those reports, which contain nothing about you. If statistics are off, none of this happens.

“Write to us”. If you send us a message from Settings, we receive the message, its type (bug, idea or other), the app and system details above, the install ID (so we can match a bug to what happened), and your email address only if you choose to give one so we can reply. Messages are kept for 24 months. This works even when statistics are off, because you send it on purpose.

Our server runs on Cloudflare, with the database in the European Union. Cloudflare processes the data for us as a service provider. We do not sell data, share it with advertisers or data brokers, or use it to track you across other apps or websites.

What we never collect

No name, no contacts, no location, no photos, no microphone, no camera, no advertising identifier and no device identifier. The app has no account or sign-in, no third-party analytics or crash reporting service, no Game Center or other leaderboard, and no social integrations. Versions up to 1.0.1 send nothing at all.

What the app stores on your device

A handful of small JSON files in the app’s own documents folder:

  • career.json — your current career: your car, parts, cash and progress.
  • records.json — your personal records and Grand Prix results.
  • legacy.json — your hall and trophies, kept across careers.
  • settings.json — your Challenge level and whether haptics and sound are on.
  • meta.json — the cosmetics you own and your season track progress.

None of these files contains your name, contact details or a device identifier, and none of them is sent anywhere. Small files next to them hold the random install ID, your statistics on/off choice and the events waiting to be sent.

Device backups

Because the saves live in the app’s documents folder, they are included in your phone’s normal backup (device or iCloud backup on iOS, Android’s own backup on Android). Those backups are handled by Apple and Google under their own privacy policies and your own backup settings. We cannot see them.

No ads, no tracking

Rocket Rover has no ads. The app contains no ad SDK and no tracking of any kind, and it never reads your device’s advertising identifier.

Purchases

Rocket Rover sells a few one-time purchases: Rocket Rover Pro, the Season Pass (season 1, cosmetic) and cosmetic packs. No coins, currency or race advantages are sold. Payment is handled entirely by Apple through the App Store (StoreKit), or by Google Play Billing on Android. We never see your card or payment details; the app only receives a receipt saying which product you own. Restore purchases is available in Settings and in the Store.

The rating prompt

The game may occasionally ask the operating system to show its standard “rate this app” sheet (StoreKit’s review request on iOS). That sheet is drawn and run by Apple or Google, not by us; any rating or review you leave goes to the store under its own privacy policy.

Haptics and sound

Haptics only use your phone’s vibration engine. Both haptics and sound can be turned off in Settings.

Deleting your data

Progress lives only on your device. Uninstalling Rocket Rover deletes the app’s documents folder and every save file with it, along with the install ID and any events still waiting. To have the statistics and messages from your install deleted from our server, email us and we will help you find your install and erase it. Start over in Settings ends the current career. Your phone’s backup may still hold a copy after you uninstall; that is cleared in your device’s backup settings. Purchases stay tied to your Apple or Google account and can be restored.

Children

Rocket Rover is a general-audience game and is not directed at children under 13. The anonymous statistics identify no one, of any age. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children; if a child sends us a message with an email address, a parent can ask us to delete it.

Your rights

If you are in the EU/EEA or UK, the GDPR gives you rights to access, correct, export and delete personal data, to object to its use, and to complain to a supervisory authority (in Estonia, the Andmekaitse Inspektsioon). The statistics are anonymous and we cannot link them to you on our own, but if you give us your install’s details or the email you wrote from, we will act on any request. For purchases, contact Apple (or Google Play on Android).

Changes to this policy

If the app starts doing something this page does not describe — ads, accounts, cloud save, online play — this page is updated first, with a new date at the top, and the store privacy answers are updated with it.
